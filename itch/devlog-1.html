Post title: Two battleships and one or two carriers: an estimate, checked against the fleet's diary. Type: Game Design (or Update). Image: itch\screenshot-7-fleet.png (the fleet at anchor) or screenshot-4-file.png. Open this file in the browser and copy from the first paragraph down.

On 16 April 1944 the Luftwaffe Operations Staff sent a short study up the chain of command: where to use the Mistel, the pilotless Ju 88 bomber steered by a fighter on its back. It survives on microfilm T-321 in the US National Archives, roll 10, in the annex volume of the staff's war diary. Its covering memo makes the whole plan rest on one sentence: "In Scapa … no more than at most 2 battleships and 1–2 carriers will be found." Since one hit was reckoned to destroy a battleship, two Mistel against each heavy ship would do. Eight in all; seven more kept back for a second attack.

The memo does not say where the estimate comes from. The study only asks, further on, for a reconnaissance flight before the attack, so that the crews can steer by photographs "on which the position of the ships is marked".

The other side kept a diary too. The war diary of the Commander-in-Chief, Home Fleet, lists for every month where each ship lay on each day. Set beside the German estimate, it gives a strange result. In the second half of May 1944, a month after the study, Scapa Flow held between four and seven battleships and up to three fleet carriers on any one day. In the second half of June, when the last of the fifteen Mistel were due, the carriers Victorious and Indomitable had sailed for the Far East and other ships had gone south for the landing in France: two battleships and one carrier, exactly what the memo expected. The estimate was right by accident, for the one fortnight in which it mattered.

Ten months later, in February 1945, the plan came back. The staff's war diary records three entries on "Unternehmen Drachenhöhle", Operation Dragon's Lair: on 12 February Göring put off the decision for three days, on the 13th it was "postponed for the time being", on the 16th it was "not to be carried out for the time being", and the fuel for it, stored in Norway, stayed blocked. The diary does not name the target. A British interrogation report of January 1946 does: the officer who was to direct it from Tirstrup in Jutland said it was meant to destroy "units of the British fleet in SCAPA FLOW", and that bad weather ended it. On the day of Göring's decision, the Home Fleet's diary shows one battleship in the Flow: the Rodney.

In the study you can read all of this as a dossier, with the file in facsimile and translation, or take the staff's seat. The model sends your Mistel against the ships the war diary places in the anchorage on your date, and counts what the file counted: hits. Two chapters count what it did not: the barriers built after the Royal Oak was sunk inside the Flow in 1939, and the Italian prisoners of war who built them, who lived in camps at the edge of the anchorage while the planners in Germany drew up their arithmetic.
